Appendix

Method, sources and limits of the readings

This appendix sets out the sources used, the procedures of transcription and analysis, the notation conventions and the limits of the readings. The readings on the analysis page rest solely on a transcription of mm.1–175 made from the printed score of 1902, without recordings or secondary literature.

What this is

What this analysis is

The analysis page records the notes that actually sound in the score, quarter-note beat by quarter-note beat. For each beat it reads a chord, a harmonic function and a tonal context. The beat readings are gathered into a three-level hierarchy of phrases, paragraphs and theme blocks. Chord labels were assigned from the sounding notes and the lowest note. The decisions to leave passing and neighbor notes out of the labels, the functional readings and the formal divisions are my analysis.

It covers mm.1–175 of the first movement: the introduction, the whole of the exposition and the three bars taken to be the start of the development. The source PDF ends at m.175, and so does the analysis. The rest of the development and movements II–IV have not yet been transcribed or analyzed.

Sources

Sources

The only primary source is a 15-page scan of the full score, and the analysis rests on the MuseScore transcription made from it. The edition it follows is the Breitkopf & Härtel score of 1902 (plate number 1739/30747). The scan was made from the 1993 Dover reprint. No secondary sources, such as recording notes or published analyses, were used.

Transcription

Transcription: method and limits

The notes were not obtained by optical recognition: all 175 bars were read staff by staff from the scanned images. The reading and the checking were carried out by AI agents (Claude) following written procedures. An agent other than the one that did the reading compared the engraved transcription with the printed score bar by bar. There are 7,021 notes, not counting grace notes. The 76 corrections made in this check concerned ties, slurs, articulation, the assignment of voices, and the position of dynamics and text. No errors of pitch or rhythm were found.

As a rule, apparent errors in the printed score were left as printed. The grace note of Horns I–II in m.115 is printed as written F4 (sounding B♭3). It appears to be an error for F𝄪 (sounding B♯3). This is the only such place that concerns pitch, and as it is a grace note, it does not affect the beat analysis.

Method

Method of analysis

The notes of every quarter-note beat were extracted automatically from the transcription's MusicXML (994 beats). The sounding notes and the lowest note were recorded at the start of each beat and at every moment within a beat where the sonority changes (1,105 records in all). Grace notes are not included in this record, and all notes of transposing instruments have been converted to sounding pitch. Chord labels were assigned from the notes and the lowest note of each beat. Every label was checked mechanically to confirm that the notes it names are sounding and that its bass matches the lowest note.

The divisions into theme blocks and paragraphs, the key plan and the corresponding passages were settled as an overall plan before the beat analysis. The divisions rest on entries, rests, cadences and thematic repetition. The beat analysis and the phrase divisions were split into ten sections and carried out by AI agents (Claude) following the plan. Each section was given an independent re-check by another agent, one that had not analyzed that section. The re-check compared every label with the record of the notes and verified the grounds of each reading and the alternative readings. Terms and readings that run across sections were made consistent in one pass after the re-check.

The analyst agent and the reviewing agent are the same model and follow the same plan and conventions, so an oversight common to both cannot be caught by this re-check.

Of the 994 beats, 885 are rated high in confidence and 109 medium; none is rated low. Most of the medium beats lie in the first half of the second group, where the bass rests and the F♯ pedal is supplied in the reading. The next largest group consists of beats over the G–B tremolo where the melody notes can be read in more than one way. Confidence is not shown on the page but is kept in the analysis data.

Conventions

Notation conventions

The notation conventions are collected under “How to read the table” on the analysis page, so only those that underlie every reading are repeated here. All pitch names are sounding pitches. The pitch name after a slash is the actual lowest note, whatever instrument plays it. Every note a label names is sounding at that moment.

Limits

Limits of the readings

The formal divisions, the functional reading of each beat and the phrase boundaries are the results of my analysis of the transcription. They do not represent the composer's intentions or the views of other analysts. The notes a label names are restricted to notes that actually sound, but in some places the functional reading leaves room for other possibilities. Those places are noted in the beat's comment or in the Tonal context column.

The source PDF ends in the third bar of rehearsal I (m.175). The reading of mm.173–175 as the start of the development was made without seeing what follows. Nor has it been checked whether the tonic and the dominant are connected in root position after this point.
